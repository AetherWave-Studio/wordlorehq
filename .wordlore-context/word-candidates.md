# Wordlore Word Candidate Pool

Pre-vetted words ready to be written into episodes. Each one has been checked against the four selection criteria: strong recognition, surprising payoff, concrete imagery, one-line "X literally means Y" format.

## Tier 1 — Visceral / shocking payoffs

These have the strongest "wait, what?" reactions and are good for high-leverage slots (launch week, new viewers, end-of-month wrap-ups).

| Word | Payoff | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **SINISTER** | Latin "left" — the left hand was the unlucky/evil hand | Strong concrete imagery |
| **AVOCADO** | Aztec Nahuatl word for testicle | Crowd-pleaser, viral potential |
| **MUSCLE** | Latin "little mouse" (they look like mice running under skin) | Visual; instantly memorable |
| **LUNATIC** | Latin "moon-struck" | Pairs well with NIGHTMARE thematically |
| **WEIRD** | Old English *wyrd* = fate, destiny | Connects to Macbeth's "weird sisters" |
| **HOOLIGAN** | Likely from a rowdy Irish family in 1890s London | Specificity makes it memorable |

## Tier 2 — Surprising historical origins

Strong payoffs with rich historical context. Good for the main rotation.

| Word | Payoff | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **COMPANION** | Latin "one you share bread with" (com + panis) | Warm, intimate origin |
| **SALARY** | Latin "salt money" — Roman soldiers paid in salt | The "worth your salt" connection |
| **QUARANTINE** | Italian "forty days" — plague ship isolation period | Timely; familiar word |
| **SARCASM** | Greek "to tear flesh" | Brutally direct |
| **BARBARIAN** | Greek mimicking foreign speech ("bar bar bar") | Linguistic xenophobia |
| **PANIC** | From the Greek god Pan, who caused irrational fear | Strong mythological hook |

## Tier 3 — Religious / mythological roots

Words secretly carrying ancient religion and myth.

| Word | Payoff | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **VOLCANO** | Roman god Vulcan, smith of the gods | Visual |
| **JANUARY** | Roman god Janus, two-faced (looking back and forward) | Annual relevance |
| **CEREAL** | Roman goddess Ceres, goddess of grain | Breakfast irony |
| **CUPID / CUPIDITY** | Same Latin root *cupere* = "to desire" | Two-fer; sets up "love is greed" reveal |
| **BEDLAM** | From Bethlehem Royal Hospital, an infamous London asylum | Real-place origin is satisfying |

## Tier 4 — Words with old surprise meanings

Modern words whose origins are unexpectedly literal or specific.

| Word | Payoff | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **CLUE** | A ball of thread (from Theseus's labyrinth) | Already chained as episode 5 |
| **TANTALIZE** | From Tantalus, eternal punishment by unreachable food/water | Strong mythological hook |
| **HALCYON** | Greek mythological bird that calmed the seas | "Halcyon days" callback |
| **NOSTALGIA** | Greek "ache to return home" (nostos + algos) | Emotional resonance |
| **TARANTULA** | From Taranto, Italy — where the dance "tarantella" comes from (folk cure for spider bites) | Three-layer payoff |

## Themed weeks (potential)

Once the channel has rhythm, consider thematic weeks for stronger algorithmic signal:

- **"Words that used to be people"**: Sinister, Lunatic (sort of), Tantalize, Bedlam, Hooligan
- **"Words for things we now have machines for"**: Robot, Salary, Quarantine
- **"Words from religions you don't follow"**: January, Cereal, Cupid, Volcano, Panic
- **"Words that mean food but didn't"**: Avocado, Companion, Salary
- **"Words about losing your mind"**: Lunatic, Bedlam, Hysteria, Panic

## Rejected candidates (and why)

To save research time later, words that didn't pass the bar:

- **TELEPHONE** — "far + sound" — accurate but unsurprising; no dramatic shift
- **AUTOMOBILE** — "self + movable" — same problem
- **PETRICHOR** — too obscure; most viewers won't recognize the word
- **DEFENESTRATION** — beloved by word nerds but most viewers don't know it
- **ANTIDISESTABLISHMENTARIANISM** — recognizable but the etymology is dull (just stacked prefixes/suffixes)

## How to use this list

1. When sitting down for Sunday production, pick 4 words from this pool for the coming week
2. Write the input JSON for each (following `production-workflow.md`)
3. Remove used words from this list; add new candidates as research surfaces them
4. Try to vary the languages of origin (don't do four Latin episodes in a row — mix in Old English, Greek, Czech, Aztec, etc. for tonal variety)
